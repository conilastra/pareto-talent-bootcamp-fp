## Frameworks and models

### 1. Busy-street analogy for traffic
- Traffic is like foot traffic on a busy street. "These people have different intentions." Assuming all of them are your ICP is "a very wrong idea".
- The ways a store pulls people in each map to online channels:
  - The sign or banner on the building is a paid placement or ad.
  - The promoter handing out flyers is cold outreach ("will work probably in, like, 1% of cases, maybe less").
  - The woman carrying a Tesco bag is an unpaid ambassador, the same as organic word of mouth.
- "These are real people with real intentions, real emotions, real desires."

### 2. Paid vs Organic
- **Organic ("free")**: social accounts, viral posts, SEO. "Not actually free": it costs time, salaries, agencies, videographers and tools. "The only truly free marketing is you personally posting in your own spare time." Even then: "If your time is worth $20 an hour, and you spend 5 hours recording content… it costed you $100." Organic is a "long-term investment play" that "will not convert immediately".
- **Paid**: ads on Facebook, Google and YouTube. "What you are paying for is placement and the platform… They will not make anything happen for you." Paid is not a "panacea". A common mistake is to think "I will just run some ads, and… we'll be good."

### 3. Outbound vs Inbound × Cold vs Hot (four quadrants)
- **Outbound**: you reach them first. **Inbound**: they reach you first, through search, a referral or content.
- **Cold**: no prior relationship. **Hot**: already aware and already warm.
- **Outbound + cold**: the flyer guy on a random street, or messaging strangers "and hope that one out of the hundred will reply".
- **Outbound + hot**: the same promoter working a handbag conference. Online, this means reaching out to subscribers or commenters. Pareto example: "hey, I saw you're interested in 5 ways to delegate. Do you have an executive assistant yourself, or have you looking to hire somebody?"
- **Inbound + hot**: Google search for you, or a referral.
- **Inbound + cold**: someone already shopping who shows a purchase intent signal ("I see your purchase from Coach, have you considered Adidas?").
- Rule: "always think, okay, is it hot or is it cold? Can I reach out to them, or do I need them to come to me first."

### 4. TOFU / MOFU / BOFU funnel
- Buyers sit at different awareness stages. The teaching example is a dentist:
  - **BOFU**: the person has the pain and is ready to buy.
  - **MOFU**: the person is considering preventive care.
  - **TOFU**: the person is unaware and has no pain.
- People **enter at any stage**, not only at the top. "Instead of you only optimizing for that, you need to be optimizing for each stage." The funnel narrows because of conversion rates.
- At each stage, the customer has different concerns. The job is "to capture them at any point of their journey".
- **BOFU channels**: Google Ads (people searching for the service or for you, e.g. "dentist urgent near me"), referrals, warm email to past buyers.
- **MOFU channels**: Facebook/Instagram ads, LinkedIn ads, and Google searches like "how often should I check my teeth".
- **TOFU channels**: YouTube with educational content, podcasts, social media content.
- "Which one is the most important?… We need absolutely all of them." **Pareto admits the gap**: "Pareto Talent is facing the same thing right now… You have… just that little small thing, which is Bofu… converting great… But there is just, 3 of them." Building MOFU takes it "not 3, but 5". Adding TOFU makes it "not 5, but 50".
- Unproven or new products have to build TOFU awareness first. Ivan's examples are Kickstarter products and Omi.me, an AI pendant marketed with funny videos.

### 5. The lead journey: 8 places to lose the lead (slide)
1. **Sees the ad**: bad timing, wrong platform, the audience isn't online.
2. **Clicks the ad**: weak creative, looks scammy, a typo.
3. **Loads the page**: slow, a broken link, a 404, the page cut off.
4. **Reads the content**: confusing layout, copy too long, poor structure, all caps, an unreadable background.
5. **Clicks the CTA**: broken button, invisible pop-up, wrong link.
6. **Submits the form**: too long, or too short to qualify anyone, or it doesn't fire.
7. **Lands on the Thank You page**: no page, no next step, buyer's remorse.
8. **Tracked as conversion**: no tracking, so "flying completely blind" and "Facebook will not even know that they converted."
- "The whole game about digital marketing" is raising the percentage that passes each step. "AI will take care of majority of these problems."

### 6. The Thank You Page (4 elements)
"E-commerce does this better than almost anyone." Every thank-you page needs four things:
1. **Confirmation**: proof that the action or payment worked.
2. **Next Steps**: what happens next.
3. **Support**: a channel to contact support.
4. **Objection-Handling**: anything that removes buyer's remorse.
- "The best thank you page is the page that actually delivers you something immediately." Pareto example: the **Hire book** lead magnet. The landing page says "get the book for free", followed by a form with "a lot of questions, because this is free". The thank-you page then says "check your email, you got it."
- Ties to **time to value**: "Thank You Page is your first way to start delivering this value."
- **Buyer's remorse** is "this feeling of regret or anxiety when you just paid for something." The green-jeans story: a salesperson resolves the regret on the spot.

### 7. Personal brand as the moat
- A **moat** is "this water that separates a castle from everyone else… an unfair advantage."
- Slide: "A competitor can copy your offer. They can't copy your face." Pricing gets undercut and features get cloned within a quarter. "A founder who has spent a year being visibly, consistently themselves in front of the exact right audience" is the moat. "Content and product used to be separate. For a founder, they're the same asset now."
- Door analogy (slide): "A product is a door anyone can copy the lock on. A personal brand is the specific person people already trust standing behind that door."
- Ivan in class: "They cannot copy your identity… your voice… your values." His story is Coca-Cola and Santa. Coke didn't sell in winter, so they built a brand figure around winter. (Told as Ivan stated it.)

### 8. Audience & competitor research

Ivan pastes sales-call transcripts into Claude, where the second brain is loaded, to extract "pain points… frustrations, why they decided to move with us." "What would happen if I would populate 100 of them? Then I would get some real result."

- If there is no base, run a **focus group**. This is the "old way".
- Other sources are forums like Reddit ("where to hire executive assistant"), YouTube comments (e.g. on "How to delegate inbox to… VA" videos) and review sites. AI can do this semi-automatically, but the prompt has to be specific. Asking generically to "Find me my ICP" fails.

- **Alex Hormozi** ads use a call-out hook: "Business owners" (later "BUSINESS OWNERS" in caps). Earlier versions called out each persona: carpet cleaners, HVAC, "Are you a web design agency owner trying to get out of the sales seat?", "Are you a financial planning firm?". "Find them and call them out." Ivan later said Hormozi "is running bad ads on purpose, that's why he's a bad example" for visual style.
- **Bare BOFU ads** like "Generate any visuals" don't name a pain or a persona: "This is just you shouting that I will sell a garage."

### 9. Scripting: three data findings *(slides only)*
1. **Hook length doesn't matter.** Short and long hooks convert about the same.
2. **Numbers in the hook underperform.** A dollar figure or percentage in the first line underperforms a plain sentence. "Save the number for later in the piece."
3. **Point the negation at the reader, never at a trend.** "You're doing X wrong" works. "Everyone thinks X, but..." doesn't.

### 10. ICP (Ideal Customer Profile)
- Slide: "one paragraph, memorized… short enough to say out loud and specific enough to disqualify most people who read it. It names who the offer is for, what revenue floor they sit above, and who actually makes the buying decision."
- **Pareto ICP example (slide)**: "Founders running a business past a real revenue floor, who are drowning in admin, and who are personally the bottleneck." "It already tells you who to stop chasing."
- Ivan's definition: "This is customer that we love, that we want, that we need." **ICP vs CP**: everyone who buys is the CP. The ICP is the repeat, high-LTV customer. In the dentist example, the ICP is people investing in whitening and check-ups, not "the teenager who just broke his teeth on the football".
- **Three ways to find the ICP:**
  1. Ask customer support "who do we love, who do we hate?" ("We love Marcus… pays on time, never asks stupid questions.")
  2. Look at **LTV** (lifetime value).
  3. Analyze the client base.
- **TAM** (Ivan glosses it as "Target Addressable Market", usually called Total Addressable Market): dentist in Phoenix about 100,000; Nike about 100 million; **executive assistants about 3 million businesses, with an ICP of about 1 million** (see numbers below). A smaller TAM isn't bad: "competition is smaller… The price that we can charge for the service is higher because there is less supply." The goal is to balance TAM size against price.

### 11. Client words, not marketer words
- Slide: "Never invent the phrasing. Extract it." Keep a running bank of the exact words real people use to describe their pain, in their own voice, from real calls. Pull from that bank first for headlines, hooks and ads.
- "'I'm the bottleneck in my own company' converts better than any marketer's paraphrase… because it's the exact sentence our founders already say to themselves." See the verbatim pain language below.

### 12. Persona → Pain → Objection → Language (slide)
- "Every real ad starts with who's actually watching, defined by an underlying cause, not a demographic. Then what they actually feel. Then why they haven't fixed it yet. Then the exact words they'd use." "Miss one link, and it's decoration, not an ad."
- **Ad batch**: "Start from the coverage gap… the persona and pain nobody's made an ad for yet, and build several genuinely different angles against it at once. That set is the batch, not one idea with the hooks rotated."
- **Personas × pains × angles board** (dentist demo):
  - Business owner (conferences) needs "be presentable", "close deals with your shiny teeth".
  - Social media influencer wants "get more followers by having whiter teeth".
  - Football player only wants to fix broken teeth.
  - Airsoft player: "get a BB out of your teeth".
  - "These angles is what will become your ads… allow you to continue talking about the same thing over and over… in different words." Once you understand them, "you will have unlimited number of the ads."

### 14. The 4-Gate Idea Test: Stage One, Fast Kill *(slides only)*
1. **One message**: can you state the core idea without an "and"? Two messages means two pieces of content.
2. **Pain point**: does it name a real pain in the viewer's words?
3. **Broad appeal**: does it land for a wide audience, or does it need niche insider knowledge in the first seconds?
4. **Curiosity hook**: does it open a loop immediately? "A flat statement of fact is not a hook."

### 15. The 7-Factor Pareto Score (0–3 each): Stage Two, Rank the Survivors *(slides only)*
1. **View Potential**: judging by the hook alone, could this reach past the usual niche?
2. **Proven Concept**: has something like it already worked?
3. **Intrigue**: does it open a loop across visual, verbal and on-screen text together?
4. **ICP Resonance**: does it name the exact pain in their own language?
5. **Payoff**: "The five-dollar test: would someone actually pay for this piece?"
6. **Pre-Selling Power**: does it shift a belief or kill an objection before the sales call?
7. **Brand Alignment**: true, on-values, defensible. "Carries a veto: score low here and nothing else matters."
- **Decision rule (max 21):** 16+ ships ("script it and produce it"). 12–15 means repair it (fix the one weak factor and rescore). 11 or under means bank it ("not dead, it's just not today… can score completely differently against a different format later"). "It's what keeps volume from turning into noise."

### 16. Ad structure (6 elements; slide numbering 1-2-3-4-5-6)
1. **Hook**: "A bold result or a sharp question in the first line." It is "what stops the person… usually… some sort of a pattern interrupt." "Hook is the most important stuff." The best method is to analyze what's working and port the format, e.g. "POV, you pitch your fiance an idea, and he secretly builds the entire thing" becomes "POV, your husband went to this clinic and now he looks nothing like he used to."
2. **Offer**: stated plainly, "with whatever risk-reversal makes it easy to say yes."
3. **Qualifier**: "Who this is for, named specifically, so the wrong person self-selects out."
4. **Proof**: "Names, numbers, results. Specific beats vague."
5. **Mechanism**: how it works, in a few steps. "Mechanism comes much, much later."
6. **CTA**: "One clear next step. Never more than one." "Without the call to action, you don't have an ad."
- Compress all six "in as short a period of time as possible".
- **Ivan's live Pareto ad walkthrough:**
  - **Weak hook**: "Do you need an executive assistant?" ("it's just a question").
  - **MOFU hook**, agency-specific: "Have you wondered how you can make your agency make more money and spend less time?"
  - **Broad TOFU hook**: "There is one way for you to spend 80% less time and make 5 times more money."
  - **Bridge**: "if you look into the Pareto distribution, 20% of your time generates 80% of the outcome. But what do you do with the rest 80% of your work? That's where your right hands comes in. They will be able to help you with your operations and admin, so you can focus on the things that actually makes you money."
  - **Qualifier**: "if you're a business owner doing less than $50,000, it's not for you." The period wasn't stated.
  - **Proof + open loop**: "After working with more than 100 entrepreneurs and saving more than 20,000 hours for them in last year alone. I noticed this one thing."
  - **Mechanism**: "We find exceptional talent from Argentina and place them with the entrepreneurs in the US."
  - **CTA**: "Do you want to see if it's the right fit for you?… Book a call below."
- Hook reuse example from the Ads Library: "Despite achieving a lot, are stress and anxiety consuming you?" Ivan would keep the same hook with a different mechanism: "get yourself a right hand that, pushes, together with you, removing the anxiety from you."

### 17. Meta Ads vs Google Ads
- Slide: "Facebook is a billboard on a street someone happens to walk down. Google is a store they just walked into, asking for exactly this."
- **Meta** is interest-based demand generation. **Google** is intent-based demand capture.
- Coverage claim: Meta (Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp) covers about 70% of the market and YouTube/Google adds about 20%. "Covering just Google and Meta Ads is more than enough." TikTok and Pinterest are optional supplements.
- Targeting has changed. Detailed interest stacking ("Harry Potter… Rolls Royce") "it's not working anymore". Now, use the standard settings: "Your ad will tell Facebook who you want." If an ad says "business owners", Facebook finds business owners. "If your ads are good, you will get people."
- "In majority of these platforms, your main goal is just not screw up."
- On Google Ads in saturated markets, the highest LTV wins the keyword auction: "If you can make more money on the person than your competitor, then you can spend not $10… but $50."

### 18. Ad account structure (same on every platform)
- **Account**: billing.
- **Tracking**: Pixel, now called "dataset".
- **Campaigns**: one per goal (sales, engagement, leads).
- **Ad sets / ad groups**: targeting, i.e. location, audience and platform.
- **Ads**: multiple headlines, creatives and URLs.
- "Campaign and ad set are technicalities"; the ad is what matters.

### 19. Metrics that matter (slide + class)
- **CTR**: clicks ÷ impressions.
- **Conversion rate**: conversions ÷ visitors.
- **CAC**: all-in cost to acquire one paying customer. Example: a $95 workshop with a CAC of $130 is "losing money".
- **ROAS**: revenue ÷ ad spend. Above 100% makes money.
- **LTV:CAC**: "The number that actually matters most. Aim to keep this ratio above three to one."
- **CPC**: cost per click.

### 20. Budget math (worked in a spreadsheet)
- **Formula**: budget = (customers ÷ conversion rate [÷ each later-stage rate]) × CPC.
- **Dentist example** ($300 price, $50 cost):
  - At $1 CPC, 10 customers cost about $218 in ads for $3,000 revenue, a ROAS of about 1,300%.
  - At $4 CPC, the ROAS is about 300%, $4,000 buys 50 customers, and CAC is about $87.
  - Adding a 20% appointment-booking step pushes CAC to about $437 to earn $300, so it "now I'm losing money."
- For a brand-new campaign: "get a certain budget, like $1,000… spend $1,000, no matter what… and then we'll see." Collecting data gives "predictability in the marketing."

### 21. Tracking across all platforms *(slides only)*
- **GA4** is the "single source of truth" for on-site behavior and conversions.
- **Google Tag Manager** is the deployment layer, so tracking changes don't require code changes.
- **Meta Pixel + Conversions API**: run both, because iOS privacy changes and ad blockers mean the Pixel alone misses conversions.

### 22. Continuity (ad ↔ landing page)
- What the ad promises must be true on the page. Example: the ad says "$97 only" and the page says $150. "The main idea… between the ad and the website is continuity." Homework ads must point to the student's website and offer.

## Jargon and terms
- **TOFU / MOFU / BOFU**: top, middle and bottom of funnel ("ToF, MoF, BoF" in the homework).
- **Moat**: an uncopyable advantage, here the founder's personal brand.
- **ICP / CP**: ideal customer profile vs. any customer profile.
- **TAM**: addressable market size (Ivan says "Target Addressable Market").
- **LTV**: lifetime value. **CAC**: customer acquisition cost. **CPC**: cost per click. **CTR**: click-through rate. **ROAS**: return on ad spend. **LTV:CAC**: target above 3:1.
- **Persona**: defined "by cause, not demographics". **Angle**: a different entry point into the same pain. **Coverage gap**: a persona-pain pair with no ad yet. **Batch**: several distinct angles built against one gap.
- **Hook**: a pattern interrupt or open loop. **Open loop**: a curiosity gap that keeps people watching. **Call-out**: naming the persona in the hook ("Business owners").
- **Continuity**: ad and landing page match.
