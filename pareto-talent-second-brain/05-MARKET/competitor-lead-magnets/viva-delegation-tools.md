# Viva — tools

**Correction:** earlier notes said Viva had no lead magnet. Its Resources hub has three tools and six ungated guides.

1. **Delegation Time Audit** (/resources/tools/delegation-time-audit/), verified by Conil: headline "How many hours could you win back?", subhead "Add up the low-leverage work on your plate and see the time and value a dedicated EA could return to you." Six inputs: inbox and email, scheduling and calendar, travel booking, expenses and admin, meeting prep and follow-up (all hours per week), plus your salary (USD a year). Three outputs: reclaimable hours per week, reclaimable hours per year, and the annual value of that time (salary over a 2,080-hour year, labelled an estimate). No email, no contact form, no saved record. One CTA, "Book a Call →". The tool itself is a pure calculator: instant, on-screen, anonymous.
2. **Executive Assistant Cost Calculator** (/resources/tools/executive-assistant-cost-calculator/): "What does an EA really cost?" "Compare the full year-one cost of an in-house executive assistant against a fully managed Viva EA." Inputs: in-house EA base salary (USD/yr), benefits and overhead (%), recruiting and onboarding (one-time), Viva plan (USD/mo). Outputs: in-house year one, Viva year one, "You keep" (the difference). Disclaimer: "Estimate only." CTA "Book a Call →" after "We match you with a vetted executive assistant, usually within about 24 hours of the call." Page says "Behind the Viva number is one flat fee." This is a cost-comparison tool built to make Viva look cheaper than a hire.
3. **Virtual Assistant ROI Calculator** (/resources/tools/virtual-assistant-roi-calculator/): "See the hours and dollars a dedicated EA gives back. Plug in your salary and the time you lose to work an assistant could handle, and watch the value of that time add up in your own numbers." Inputs: executive annual salary (USD), hours a week on delegatable admin, working weeks a year. Outputs: hours reclaimed a year, dollar value of that time, workdays reclaimed (8-hour days); hourly value is salary over working weeks times 40. Disclaimer: "This estimates the value of the time you would get back, not a Viva price." CTA "Book a Call" (/register).

Guides: CEO's Guide to Owning Their Schedule; How to Delegate to an Executive Assistant; How to Manage Executive Burnout; How CEOs Manage Their Time; EA vs Chief of Staff; EA Staffing Agency Alternative. Plus five "Viva vs" pages ([[viva-execviva]]).

## Why it matters
This is the nearest competitor magnet to a Pareto cost-of-your-time calculator: LatAm EAs, same cost-of-your-time logic. It appears to sell the call, not to qualify. Pareto's edge: qualify inside the tool and route straight to a calendar.


## What the audit shows (verified)
Strengths: instant number (hours and dollars) with no friction and no email; the number is the founder's own, which sells the EA. Weaknesses: no list built, no qualification (a salary field is the only signal and it is not stored), no follow-up, and one CTA for everyone, so nothing separates a $50K salary from a $500K one. This is a model for the **hours-back number** Pareto's result can show, but Pareto would capture the email first, qualify, and route.


## All three tools compared (2026-10-03)
Same pattern: instant on-screen numbers, no email, no qualifier, one button "Book a Call". They differ in the number: time audit = hours by task area plus value; ROI = value of hours using salary; cost calculator = in-house year one vs Viva year one (a price comparison, the only one that mentions Viva's cost). None captures a lead unless the visitor books, so non-bookers are untraceable. The cost calculator reveals pricing posture (one flat fee) while the other two avoid it.
