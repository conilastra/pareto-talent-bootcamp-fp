# HRIS Careers Podcast: "Hiring Remote Talent That Actually Performs: Kasim Aslam's Hire Framework"

> Full text: [[hriscareers-podcast-kasim-aslam-full-transcript]]. Not the raw transcript; quotes are verbatim from the pasted text. Host Troy Wallace met Kasim 15+ years ago (basketball court, Phoenix; Troy was HR technology director at Republic Services).

## What it is
Part one of two. The host describes Kasim as a serial entrepreneur with an eight-figure exit (Solutions 8, "largest Google ads agency in the country, maybe globally") and says Pareto Talent is "developing a global network of high-level executive assistants" ("over 100" at the time). The free book is at thehirebook.com (about 115 pages, with worksheets, templates, checklist and audiobook; asks only for an email). See [[hire-book]] and [[thehirebook]].

## Key points

**1. HR stack for global hiring (Kasim: "extraordinarily difficult")**
- No off-the-shelf tool fit; they demoed Deel and it "took a lot, offered a little." They built a **relational database in Airtable** that handles raises on time horizons, equipment purchases, promotions, and automations that reconcile with **QuickBooks**. Also looked at Grist and Smartsheet.
- Payment rails differ by country: "this country can use Payoneer but this country can only use Wise"; in some countries employees prefer US bank accounts because their economy is unstable.
- Kasim does **not** recommend this for most companies: "I own a staffing agency... I've got 100 trained [EAs] and so I'm able to spread the cost of that infrastructure across every single one of them. Most people should probably have an out-of-the-box solution" (EOR, Deel, Rippling, or a consultant).
- Compliance: uses "a very good international labor attorney" on a semi-regular basis. His summary: no trade agreement and no office in the country usually means no compliance burden on the US employer; in some countries the burden is on the employee, in some on you; some countries "want double just for the taxes"; elsewhere hire them as **contractors, not employees**, and check the boxes.
- **LLMs for legislation:** "have two entirely different LLMs do the same task and then find out where they disagree. Generally speaking, that's a hallucination." He thinks the result's "propensity for error is lower than a human attorney."
- Solutions 8: 80 employees, "six were in the United States"; it went through a SoftBank-backed buyer and Goldman Sachs-trained due diligence over nine months "without a question" on this setup.

**2. Government fees on offshore payments:** expects small transfer fees to grow ("it's like a credit card fee... gets passed on to the end customer") and says it will not stop the arbitrage.

**3. Why he hires globally even without arbitrage**
- "If God came to earth and said you have to pay every human equal, I'd still be going offshore, nearshore." Reason: in the US the "peak performers go work for Google, Amazon, Microsoft, Apple, Facebook, or they go into business for themselves... small business owners are the leftovers." Domestic entry-level staff: "the odds of them being a peak performer are literal zero" (his words, an opinion).
- Foreign employees are "grateful for the opportunity." He pays "more than they'd ever make domestically, most of the time by many multiples", lets them work from home on a semi-flexible schedule, and says this is not exploitation.
- Story: a Solutions 8 Google Ads specialist in India earned $4,500 a month versus about $500 for peers, bought his parents their first home, and managed the biggest client (a public pet-supplement company paying $54,000 a month). Kasim's impact claim: when a mother works from home "teen pregnancy drops, teen drug use drops, teen gang involvement drops" (his belief, not sourced).
- **Pareto Talent = the 80/20 people:** "which are the 8020s or the 20% of people that do 80% of the work." Solutions 8 had **40% margins in an industry that aims at 15%**: "It's not because I'm magic. It's because I had better people."
- Employer practice: pay early and on time ("I am a freaking clock"); some workers in India and Pakistan hold three full-time jobs because employers withhold pay; earn trust and they "go all in on you." Job postings: "you must comply with all rules... and if you don't you will be publicly flogged" is "their sales letter to come work here."

**4. Where Pareto finds talent now:** "Pareto is 100% in Latin America": big concentration in Argentina (arbitrage weakened by the stronger peso), then Colombia, Ecuador, Lima (Peru), some Nicaragua.

**5. The Hire framework (details in [[flytraps-and-skunks]] and [[job-post-anatomy]])**
- **Prerequisite:** pay more than anyone else in that market. "Can you get an employee in Latin America for two grand? Sure, try paying three." His personal CFO is a college professor.
- **Job posting = a sales letter** (split-tested over "thousands of iterations in 20 years").
- **Fly traps** (about 150 of them): subject line must read "I actually read the instructions"; five or six instructions such as "send me a link to your resume in a PDF format with this naming convention." "I want to know you can stop and follow instructions."
- **No filter on experience and education:** "I hire for will and train for skill unless I need a very specific skill, something like Ruby on Rails or heart surgery."
- **Paid trial before any interview:** "$40 for two hours" in Latin America ($20 in the Philippines), paid in advance by PayPal, then he waits. "I don't follow up. I wait to see who comes to me and says, 'Hey, where's the project?'" Many ghost. "Best $40 you've ever spent." Those who follow up get a "dynamite" project. "By the time somebody goes through this process, you have the greatest talent you're ever going to hire."
- Interviews hide good people: "the ones that don't look good sometimes are your best employees."
- Next book: **Delegate** (managing them properly).

## Useful for the project
- **Authority and trust angle:** Kasim as a hiring-systems operator, not only a marketer; a proof point that Pareto's selectivity is a deliberate filter ("1 in 1,000").
- **Contrast for the lead magnet:** most founders hire like "commodities" and fish in the domestic pool; the funnel can offer the shortcut.
- **Caveat:** several statements are opinions or unsourced stories (peak-performer claims, social effects). Do not use them as facts in public assets. Not a Pareto sales call; no new client words.
