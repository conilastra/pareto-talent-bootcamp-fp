# BELAY — The Financial Advisor's Delegation Guide

## Format
6-page branded PDF. Page 1 cover, pages 2-4 three exercises, pages 5-6 BELAY pitch and CTA.
## Promise
Landing page: "Stop Losing Your Best Hours to Admin Work and Client Follow-Up". Subhead: "Reclaim 10+ hours/week and stop being the bottleneck in your firm." Three bullets: identify high-leverage tasks to eliminate, a workflow checklist to minimise rework and delays, a process for responsive, consistent client contact. Trust badges: Chick-fil-A, Maui Jim, Michael Hyatt & Co.
## Who it's for
Financial advisors, RIAs, wealth managers, planners. A **vertical version** of BELAY's generic delegation guide ([[belay-delegation-worksheet-guide]]), same topic, different niche.
## How they capture
Email only, button "Get the Free Guide". Much lighter than the 12-field form on the generic worksheet. Below the form: "About BELAY" (15+ years, nearly 2,000 US-based professionals, match in about a week).
## How they qualify
Not at all. The niche is the qualifier: only advisors self-select into it.
## What's inside the PDF
- **Framing:** "Delegation isn't just for the overwhelmed. It's a strategic move for growth-oriented financial advisors."
- **Exercise 1, the 80/20 rule:** your 20% = client meetings, portfolio reviews, prospecting, strategy; the time-stealers = scheduling quarterly reviews, CRM notes, chasing paperwork, platform/vendor emails. "Important, but not worth your billable rate."
- **Exercise 2, the 6 T's:** Tiny, Tedious, Time-consuming, Teachable, Terrible at, Time-sensitive, each with advisor examples.
- **Exercise 3, Delegation Matrix:** love/hate x important/unimportant -> Keep / Release / Trust / Forget.
- **Next steps (sales pitch):** "Now comes the part most advisors get wrong: who you trust with it." BELAY Client Services Assistants: US-based, background-checked and identity verified; experienced in wealth management ops; fluent in secure systems and compliance. Four benefits (out of the scheduling, inbox and CRM bottleneck; hand off onboarding end-to-end; ramp up in surge seasons without W-2 overhead; focus on clients and growth). CTA: "If you're tired of being the bottleneck... we should talk." Tagline: "Elite support for financial advisors who refuse to be buried in admin."
## Strengths
Frictionless (email only); a real method (three quick exercises) with no signup-for-a-sales-pitch feel; written in the buyer's own vocabulary (billable rate, compliance, client trust); the pitch reframes "who you trust with it" as the real problem, which flows naturally into the hire; vertical focus builds relevance and reuse across industries.
## Weaknesses
No personal result: the exercises are self-scored on paper, nothing is calculated. No qualification, so no routing; CTA is a vague "we should talk" with no booking link inside the PDF. Cost: BELAY's US-based talent is positioned on compliance and trust, not savings, and the PDF doesn't say what it costs.
## What Pareto can do better
Copy the **format logic** (short, three exercises, 80/20 and a matrix, then "who you trust with it") but make it personal and measurable: a founder-specific delegation score or hours-back number, 2-3 qualifying questions, and a booking link for qualified founders. Consider a niche or ICP angle in the title (BELAY proves that vertical versions work), e.g. for founders at a revenue stage. The "who you trust" bridge is the best part of the PDF: Pareto's equivalent is trained, vetted Right Hands with a Lifetime Replacement guarantee. See [[competitive-landscape]].
