## 1. Business Mapping (method → [[business-mapping]])

- Ivan: a business map is "a flow chart indicating all of the different steps in the business linking to particular automations, processes, etc." Draw it **from the client's point of view**, left to right: traffic → landing page → form → qualifier (diamond, yes/no) → emails → sales call, and so on.
- Only map the traffic sources the business actually uses.
- Every step must link to the real asset: landing page URL, form, the SOP that defines "qualified", the email template, the sales deck, the contract, the invoice.
- Examples shown: the process map for the HIRE book launch (publisher in China, 300 or 500 copies, shipping, workflow page, landing page, thank-you page, nurture sequence) and the full Pareto Talent map (blue = bootcamp candidates, red = entrepreneurs; Facebook ads, social, Reddit, referrals → step-one form → automations → scoring approved or denied → bootcamp onboarding, agenda, attendance and homework forms, final project form, feedback form → CSM interview → Pareto onboarding → "limbo state" waiting for placement → matching → interview → kickoff).
- Why Pareto does it: "without that we are just flying blind"; lets them redesign on purpose (e.g. move the TSM interview before the final project); once a year they re-check the map and simplify it; marks "risk zones" to improve.
- Tool: **Draw.io** (free, lives in Google Drive). Miro's free tier gives 3 boards.
- Six stages "more or less applicable for all businesses": traffic, capture, qualify and nurture, sales/money/handoff (slide groups Stages 5-7), onboarding through 90 days (Stages 8-11), retain/grow/exit (Stages 12-14). Slide: "One universal shape, every business fits it."
- **10-second rule:** can you name the document, process, link or automation for this step within ten seconds? If not, it is a gap. Never invent a plausible link or SOP title. Gaps expose **key-man risk** (one person, often a long-time developer, holds the keys).
- Exit logic: "Exit is the ultimate test if what you build actually works." A mapped business with SOPs and automations is one somebody else can buy. Kasim "sold three companies" (Ivan, spoken; see [[open-questions]] #7, #16).
- How a new Right Hand maps an unknown business: interview the people who own each process, record the conversations, let AI assemble the flowchart, then hand it to the founder with the blanks marked. Ivan has a Claude skill for this; slides call it the **Business Map Kit**, shared with all Right Hands in week one. Five outputs, incl. a **Gap Register** and a **Delegation Plan** addressed to the Right Hand.
- Link to the project: everything built in the bootcamp already fits the map (traffic sources, landing page, form, automation, next steps). Ivan starts a Business Map before starting a business.
- Students who missed it: it is the first ~20 minutes of the Day 10 recording.

## 2. Inside Pareto Talent (values, expectations, dealbreakers)

Numbers on the slides ([[proof-and-numbers]]): 100+ founders served · 93% still together at 12 months · **$2,192,228** paid in salary to the network · 250+ remote professionals · 100+ recorded trainings. Ivan: salaries paid "in Argentina, Colombia, Bolivia, etc.", "in the last two and a half years, more or less".

**Who they want:** independent problem solvers, not task-takers; people who hold themselves to a high standard, see a gap and close it, never need chasing. Not a quick gig.

**G.R.O.W.T.H. core values** (signed in the contractor agreement; full detail in [[growth-values]]): Growth through feedback · Respect boundaries · Own and lead boldly · Win and have fun together · Teach and grow · Honesty first.

**Three expectations** (slide order; Ivan names the second "productivity"): 1) **Communication**, say where things stand before anyone asks, bad news loudest; 2) **Proactivity**, bring the answer, or bring options with the question; 3) **Technical self-reliance**, Google, then Claude, then YouTube, then the community. Ivan on proactivity: 9 of 10 tasks done perfectly is a "pass" at university but useless to a founder who cannot tell which one is broken; "the best person will do 11".

**Four dealbreakers:** lack of initiative ("AI will sit down and wait to be told", so a task-taker adds nothing over AI) · dishonesty · inconsistency · breaking confidentiality. Ivan can name ~3 cases of firing for dishonesty.

## 4. What happens after the bootcamp

Four steps (slide): **Final Project** (80%+, i.e. 20/25, reviewed by a person) → **CSM interview** (one-on-one with Client Success; looks at the project, live communication, values, availability, fit with founders; "Passing the project gets you the interview. It doesn't get you in.") → **Pareto onboarding** (Handbook signed, Pareto Talent email account, access to recordings, Slack, internal courses) → **stay active**.

- "We can't guarantee a job. We can guarantee an interview, if your project earns it."
- Keep your Pareto calendar availability updated. CSMs must find a 30-minute slot in a founder's packed calendar; silence loses placements. Just before this bootcamp they **offboarded 20+ inactive people**.
- Continuous education while waiting for a match.
- Interview is a normal get-to-know-you chat; "no trick questions", "not a background check" (Mrika). CSM team: no averages exist for pass rate; "prepare as much for the final project as you would for that interview" (Carolina).
- Pool: ~30 graduates currently unplaced. Not a competition; matching needs variety of personalities and skills. No fixed time limit in the pool, but repeated failed interviews (4-5) mean they will tell you it is not a good opportunity; coaches debrief interviews.
- Bootcamps run about 3-4 times a year, "whenever we are short on people to place"; the last was in April.
- Funnel since the start: **35K+ applicants → 2,000+ finished → 350 accepted to Pareto Talent**. Last cohort: **10,381 applied → 709 entered bootcamp → 247 final projects → 118 became Right Hands**.
- Ivan's goal: **1,000 Right Hands** in the community (250 now).

### Pareto Handbook (six things, slide)
Professional relationship · Pareto's role (matching, interviews, prep, feedback, contracts, payments, support) · expectation alignment at **30/60/90 days** with the client, supported by the TSM · communication non-negotiable (Slack and email, flag availability and blockers) · your role (calendar updated, trainings, ownership) · transparency: placement and rematching are **not guaranteed**.

## 5. The Final Project (full brief → [[final-project-guidelines-full-text]]; clarifications → [[day-10-clarifications]])

- First time it is for **Pareto Talent itself** (earlier cohorts used a "fake but real" founder: Nuno, Joe Polish). Plan B project exists, but one scoring system only.
- IP stays with the student. If Ivan loves a lead magnet he will contact the student and **buy it for $100**; if it **beats his on ads (lower cost per lead)**, **+$300**. Slide: "If your lead magnet is cool, I'll buy it from you for $100."
- Goals: position Pareto as the authority on delegation and founder freedom; grow the list; move qualified founders to a Right Hand Program call. Ivan's motive: more founders means more placements and more bootcamp graduates passed.
- **Nine parts**: 1 Second Brain + market research (3+ source types; 3 competitor lead magnets; ICP; 3+ personas by cause in client words) · 2 Business Map · 3 ClickUp breakdown + one SOP (work backwards from Oct 7) · 4 Design the lead magnet and offer (any format; MAGIC formula; Value Equation; define "qualified"; next-step offer) · 5 Build it, live, shareable, auto-delivered by email · 6 Funnel (landing page, qualifying form, booking page, thank-you page; GHL, Lovable, Claude Code or other) · 7 CRM and workflows · 8 Five Facebook ads + AI imagery · 9 2-minute Loom.
- Part 7 detail: pipeline opted in → qualified → call booked → client, plus an unqualified stage; opt-in workflow (deliver, tag, create opportunity, notify); booking workflow (confirmation + reminder); **qualified follow-up: 3 emails**; **unqualified nurture: 1 email delivering the lead magnet**. Test the chain yourself. **Build workflows but leave them turned off** to avoid email loops.
- Deliver **one PDF**: Section 1 cover · Section 2 link index with exact labels (L1…L12; unbuilt = "L# · NOT SUBMITTED" or label plus a dash; never a fake link) · Section 3 walkthrough, one heading per part. File name `FP_FirstnameLastname_ParetoBootcamp.pdf`. Submit at bootcamp.paretotalent.com/finalproject with the registration email and a link to the PDF.
- **Automatic disqualifiers**: any link that errors, is private or needs access (test in incognito, Cmd+Shift+N) · login walls · shortened links (bit.ly etc.), write full https:// · image-only PDF (text must be selectable) · missing/renamed labels · late submission, no extensions.
- **Scoring**: five categories × 5 points = 25. Strategy & Offer (Parts 1, 4) · Lead Magnet & Funnel (5, 6) · CRM & Automation (7) · Creative & AI (8) · Project Management & Presentation (2, 3, 9). **20-25** pass: certificate, CSM interview, then Handbook and onboarding. **15-19**: certificate, no interview, can retake future cohorts. **0-14** (or no submission): no certificate, can retake. Extra-mile work counts inside the category it belongs to.
- **Dates**: deadline **EOD Oct 7** (11:59 PM Uruguay, GMT-3; Argentina same). Results and certification **EOD Oct 16**. Interviews begin the week after Day 10 (Ivan scores as submissions arrive; early submitters can be interviewed within days).
- Examples shown live: the HIRE book funnel (Facebook ad → white/blue landing page with social proof → qualifying form: business owner?, team size, revenue, assistants hired before, when needed → qualified: "your book is on the way" + VSL + call booking), the **Pareto Talent Delegation Audit** (AI quiz app that gives a delegation score; built by Gaspar), and a competitor lead-magnet funnel from the Facebook Ads Library (spreadsheet "managed 17 companies" lead magnet → qualifier questions → thank-you → booking page; opted in with a disposable email). See [[existing-lead-magnets-and-front-end-offers]].
