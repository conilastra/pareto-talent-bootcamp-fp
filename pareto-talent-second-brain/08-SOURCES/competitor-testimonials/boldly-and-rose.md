### B1. Permira
- Source: https://boldly.com/blog/how-phenomenal-executive-assistants-drive-growth-at-permira/
- Client: Kyle Butler, Investment Professional, Permira
- Industry / size: Global investment firm (private equity); "16 offices across Europe, the United States, and Asia"
- Role hired: Fractional executive assistants (subscription staffing, "a managed service that embeds fractional executive assistants alongside Permira employees"). Started with 1 EA at 20 hrs/week; expanded to "an entire team of dedicated Boldly EA's across a number of functions, including assistants with niche backgrounds in HR and their CRM". EAs described as having "10+ years of experience". Named EA: Alyssa. Location: remote, not stated further.
- Verbatim quotes:
  - "Boldly EAs can seamlessly assist four to five people, so we were able to give each of our analysts, associates, and VPs dedicated one-to-one support. Every single one of our Boldly EAs helps us be more organized and efficient. Our lives are easier, and as a team we are achieving more of our Permira objectives."
  - "One benefit of the fractional model is that it lets us ebb and flow support as demand and capacity change. When our team was growing very rapidly last year we increased Boldly hours across the board. We are also able to pare back a bit and save cost in the summer when things are a bit slower for us."
  - "I view Alyssa as a trusted partner. Say I've been emailing with a CEO or advisor and I want to schedule a meeting, I don't feel the need to be the person to engage in coordinating. I fully trust her to manage those high-level relationships and be the face of Permira."
  - "We weren't looking to compromise on quality. We came to Boldly looking for the same top-quality, premium EA talent that we hire internally. With Boldly we also get the benefit of flexibility."
- Before: (narrative) "associates and analysts were spending huge amounts of their time on admin work. Things were slipping through the cracks from time to time as their junior team didn't have dedicated support."
- Expectations: long-term admin support with subscription flexibility; premium EA talent equal to internal hires; niche skills (HR, CRM).
- After: more organized and efficient; one-to-one support for analysts/associates/VPs; scaled hours up during rapid growth, down in summer; EA trusted to handle CEO/advisor relationships. No numeric metrics.
- Worth the money: flexibility to scale hours and "save cost" in slow periods; one EA covering 4-5 people; quality equal to internal hires; trust.
- Objections/hesitations: quality was non-negotiable ("We weren't looking to compromise on quality").

### B4. "Top 3" US fast food chain (anonymous)
- Source: https://boldly.com/blog/serving-top-quality-admin-support-to-a-top-3-fast-food-chain-with-remote-eas/
- Client: unnamed Talent Management Professional "responsible for strategic external partnerships"; multinational name-brand fast food chain ("Top 3"), "year-over-year double-digit growth". Size not stated (large enterprise).
- Role hired: Remote EAs. Pilot of 3 EAs for 3 teams within a month of contact; 18 EAs for 18 teams in less than a year (talent planning, operations/finance, launch support, international restaurant development, marketing). Tasks: expense reports, travel booking, proofreading, structuring critical communications.
- Verbatim quotes:
  - "We've grown really fast and were adding new staff at all different levels. But even with so much hiring, it was still a headcount prioritization game — teams were struggling to justify a full-time administrative assistant when there were just so many other needs."
  - "Our team was being swamped by the pent-up administrative needs."
  - "Our policies are pretty risk averse when it comes to co-employment; we have a 12-month policy on temporary workers. So by the time you hire somebody and get them up to speed, they're actually effective at about month four. Then six or so months later, you have to start thinking about hiring and training somebody else. It was just a constant, vicious cycle."
  - "We were looking for a partner with a strong culture. We found that with Boldly, and we loved the risk mitigation. We especially liked that Boldly hires its team and how you all care for your employees."
  - "Boldly's team is excellent at what they do but they're also just wonderful as humans to be around. That makes a big difference in the work."
  - "Boldly's biggest differentiator is your team, your people. There are some alternatives out there but your hiring sets the bar and expectations really high, which fits well with us as a people-company with equally high standards."
  - "It's been a huge lift — our people can now do what they were hired to do. And it's because the Boldly team is there, fulfilling all of the other things that are necessary to get work done but can take up a lot of our team's time. Everybody's happy."

Found Boldly via Google search. "They hadn't planned on remote support".
- Expectations: cost-effective solution that matches growth; risk mitigation (co-employment); strong culture; high hiring bar.
- After: pilot to 18 EAs in under a year; leaders freed for strategic work; "significantly enhancing overall productivity" (narrative, no figures).
- Worth the money: avoids headcount fights; avoids temp turnover cycle; co-employment risk handled; people quality.
- Objections/hesitations: had not planned on remote support; started with a small pilot "to gauge whether Boldly could meet their high standards and unique needs."

## PATTERNS (13 items: Boldly 5 customer-page quotes collapsed into 5 sources [4 stories + Beamery]; Rose 4 site cards + 4 Trustpilot)
Counts are by source/client (Boldly n=5, Rose n=8 incl. Trustpilot; Rose Delgrande counted once).

Top pain points
- Executive/team overwhelmed by admin, pulled from core work: 5 (Permira, Ginkgo, Echoing Green, Fast food, Rose Brooks implied)
- Things slipping through the cracks / no dedicated support for junior staff: 1 (Permira)
- Can't justify or don't need a full-time 40-hr hire: 3 (Ginkgo, Echoing Green, Fast food)
- Bad past experience with temps/contractors/in-house (turnover, retraining cycle): 1 (Fast food)
- Painful/opaque hiring and agency pricing: 1 (Rose Pardini)

Triggers
- Rapid company growth: 4 (Permira, Ginkgo, Fast food, Beamery "continued to grow in both the US and the UK")
- Headcount constraints: 1 (Fast food)
- Need for a specific growth function (ads, SDR, account managers): 3 (Rose)

Expectations / what they looked for
- Premium quality equal to internal hires / high hiring bar: 3 (Permira, Fast food, Rose Johnson "high quality")
- Flexibility / fractional hours / scale up and down: 3 (Permira, Ginkgo, Echoing Green)
- Values/culture alignment and employer ethics (benefits, care for staff): 2 (Echoing Green, Fast food)
- Speed without a traditional hiring process: 3 (Ginkgo, Rose Del Grande, Rose Pardini)
- Risk mitigation (co-employment) / transparency on cost: 2 (Fast food, Rose Pardini)

Value drivers (results / why worth it)
- Trust: EA as "trusted partner", handles senior relationships, protects time: 3 (Permira, Ginkgo, Echoing Green)
- Peace of mind / emotional relief ("no-brainer", "cleaner and clearer", "Everybody's happy"): 3 (Ginkgo, Echoing Green, Fast food)
- Productivity: people do what they were hired to do: 4 (Permira, Ginkgo, Echoing Green, Fast food)
- Expansion as proof (land and expand: 1 to team; 3 to 18; spread "like wildfire"): 3 (Permira, Ginkgo, Fast food)
- Cost flexibility (pay only for hours needed, pare back in slow season): 3 (Permira, Ginkgo, Echoing Green)
- Hard metrics: only Rose provides them (30+ hrs/week saved; $3K to $65K ad spend; pipeline in <60 days): 3. Boldly gives zero numeric outcomes.
- Day-one competence / felt like a local employee: 2 (Rose Kendall, Rose Del Grande)

Objections/hesitations mentioned
- Quality compromise fear: 1 (Permira); remote support not planned / started with pilot: 1 (Fast food); offshore skepticism: 1 (Rose Kendall); mission fit / confidentiality worry: 1 (Echoing Green, implicit).

Observations for Pareto Talent
- Boldly sells US-based, W2, part-time fractional EAs on trust and peace of mind; no cost-vs-local comparison and no numbers. Rose sells offshore hires across many roles with numbers, but its site testimonials lack companies and mostly cannot be matched to verified reviews, and none is clearly an EA placement.
- Gap neither fills: a named, verifiable EA case study with before/after hours saved and cost vs a local hire.
