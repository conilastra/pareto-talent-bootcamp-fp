# Why Founders Hire an Executive Assistant: What Competitors' Clients Say

Analysis of 375 written testimonials and case studies from 8 Pareto Talent competitors, plus 39 transcribed video testimonials (see [[video-transcripts]]), compared with Pareto Talent's own Wall of Love (section 9). Collected 3 October 2026.

Sections 1 to 8 are about competitors' clients, not Pareto's. Quotes are copied from the competitors' pages. The full extraction, with every quote and source link, is in the second brain under 08-SOURCES/competitor-testimonials.

---

## 1. The short version

1. **Founders don't hire an EA because they want an assistant. They hire because they have become the bottleneck.** The most common story is a founder or CEO doing "everything", drowning in email and calendar, with no time for the work only they can do. In BELAY's set alone, about 60 stories open with inbox overload and about 50 with calendar chaos.
2. **The moment they act is a breaking point, not a plan.** Typical triggers: growth outran them, the last assistant or VA failed, an assistant left, or a personal moment ("up at 2 a.m. researching flights"). "Previous help failed" is BELAY's single most common trigger (about 40 stories).
3. **What they want is someone proactive they don't have to manage.** Across DonnaPro, BELAY, Viva and Boldly, the words that repeat are "proactive", "ahead of me", "see around corners", "without me having to ask", "I don't have to micromanage".
4. **What makes it worth the money is rarely the money.** The value clients describe is peace of mind, trust, headspace, time with family, a real holiday, and growth they couldn't reach alone. "I pay for peace." "You can't put a price on the peace of mind." "It wasn't even about the dollars." Only South and HireLATAM sell mainly on cost savings.
5. **The biggest objection is letting go, not price.** Fear of giving up control appears about 35 times in BELAY's stories, ahead of remote-work doubts (about 16) and cost (about 15).
6. **The gap for Pareto:** nobody combines named, believable founder stories with hard numbers. BELAY and DonnaPro have the emotion with few numbers. South has the numbers with no client voice at all. Pareto's lead magnet and proof can do both.
7. **Pareto's own Wall of Love has the same gap, and a different one.** Its 15 written quotes are almost all "after" praise of the assistant (jumps in, self-starter, reliable, can't imagine going back). One quote does carry a before and a trigger: Eric Ritter, "I've had three different executive assistants before, and I thought it was me, right? I was burning through them." The one Pareto video that could be transcribed (JV, a soccer-training owner) is the richest Pareto proof so far: it has the pain, the reason he committed, what the match felt like, and what he would tell other owners. Five Pareto videos are still untranscribed.
8. **The videos add three things the text did not show:** (a) the *moment* of breaking (a COO realising an email sat unanswered for a week and his own team was being asked to be consistent; a CEO leaving his wife's birthday party to buy her present); (b) the *buying criteria*, which are about the match and the onboarding more than about the price; (c) the *proof of value in numbers*, mostly from founders who count their own hours ("10 to 20 hours a week", "revenue almost tripled", "15 to 20% more productive", "accomplished more in a month than the six months prior").

## 3. Why they were looking for an EA: the pain points

Ranked by how often they appear across all sources. Counts are approximate.

### 3.1 "I'm doing everything myself" (the founder is the bottleneck)
The most common framing everywhere. About 40 BELAY stories, 6 DonnaPro, 5 South, plus HireLATAM and Rose.

- "We all think as business owners that we can just do it all." (Sheena Parker, founder, BELAY)
- "One of the great fallacies of being an independent practitioner [is that] I felt for a very long time that I was supposed to be able to do everything by myself." (Kevin Callahan, BELAY)
- "My mindset before was that I need to do it all... it was slowing me down and costing my business money." (Pawel Nical, founder, DonnaPro)
- "As a small business owner, we're doing way too many things. We work in the business too much and not on the business." (Rodney Finglass, co-owner, BELAY)
- "After a long time managing everything on my own in the startup world, I realized I needed help." (Jochen R., MedTech CEO, DonnaPro)

### 3.2 Email and inbox overload
About 60 BELAY stories. The numbers clients give are striking: 200–300 emails a day, 1,500 unread, 20,000 unread across 4 accounts.

- "My number one issue that I probably struggle with more than anything else is emails." (Sherri Swanson, BELAY)
- "By the time I would get to my emails, it would be in the evening, and that would take away from my family time." (Sherri Swanson, BELAY)

### 3.3 Calendar chaos
About 50 BELAY stories: double-booking, missed meetings, too many requests.

- "The number of contacts and requests to be on my calendar became overwhelming. I had too much complexity." (John Fees, CEO, BELAY)
- "Erika has helped me manage an incredibly high meeting load with more priorities than I could otherwise support." (Jennifer Chao, Retool, Viva)

### 3.4 No time for strategy, growth or "the work only I can do"
About 50 BELAY stories frame it this way, and it is the dominant reason in DonnaPro (9 of 21). This is the pain founders feel most because it costs them money.

- "As CEO of multiple companies, you need more time to think, and activities like email, calendaring, and contact management just suck time and take attention." (John Fees, BELAY)
- "We were at a point where we had to turn down new projects because I was completely bogged down by administrative bottlenecks." (Markus A., creative agency, DonnaPro)

### 3.5 Work spilling into family and personal life
About 35 BELAY stories: missed dinners, bedtimes, a wife's birthday party, strain on the marriage.

- Brad Johnson (BELAY) missed family commitments including his wife's birthday party: "Brooke makes our marriage better because we don't have the conflicts."

### 3.6 Overwhelm, stress and burnout, sometimes physical
About 35 BELAY stories, plus DonnaPro. Clients mention stress ulcers, high blood pressure, "drowning".

- "I reached out to [BELAY] as a result of me realizing the night before that I was up at 2 a.m. researching flight options and connection times." (Chris Moore, BELAY)
- "She's literally improved my health. I'm not as stressed as I was eight weeks ago." (Chris Moore, BELAY)

### 3.7 Things slipping through the cracks
About 20 BELAY stories, plus Viva and Boldly.

- "I am no longer concerned about missing things, because I know she is looking out ahead in a way I am not." (Anthony Gregorio, Veho, Viva)

### 3.8 Previous help failed (this is both a pain and the top trigger)
About 40 BELAY stories, plus DonnaPro, South and Boldly. They tried temps, freelancers, Upwork, other VA agencies, offshore VAs, or hiring on their own.

- "I've tried other administrative support programs from temps that are local to one that was an offshore virtual assistant, and this is the only thing that's worked for me." (John Fees, BELAY)
- "My history with Virtual Assistants goes back almost eight years and it's been a rollercoaster in terms of success." (Bill Edgar, BELAY)
- "That didn't work out. That was a dumpster fire." (Jasmine Womack on hiring by herself, BELAY)
- "I've been working with virtual assistants for the past decade..." (Gonçalo Crespo, DonnaPro)
- South: earlier Upwork and agency hires "required excessive hand-holding" (writing-coaching business).

### 3.9 Hiring itself is the pain
Hiring is slow, expensive, and pulls the founder away from the business. South mentions this in about 10 stories; DonnaPro and BELAY too.

- "I knew I wanted an agency to avoid the hiring hassle." (Gonçalo Crespo, DonnaPro)
- "I couldn't go out and find somebody like Leah or Hali, with their skill sets, to come to work with me." (Rodney Finglass, BELAY)

**How this lines up with Pareto's own data:** Pareto's 1,500+ sales calls rank the founder pains as admin 80%, no time 70%, burned by a VA 60%, runs from memory 50%, things slip 40%, too many hats 30%. Competitors' clients confirm every one of these. The competitor stories add two Pareto doesn't name: **family and health cost** and **turning down business**.

- The quotes are dated in the first 30–90 days, so they show a fast start but not a long-term result. Pareto's own stat (93% still together at 12 months, from the second brain) fills that.

## 9b. What the 39 transcribed videos add (BELAY 27, Boldly 6, DonnaPro 2, HireLATAM 3, Pareto 1)

Full notes: [[video-transcripts]]. About half of BELAY's videos are accounting/bookkeeping stories, not EA stories; the EA-specific points below come from the EA/VA/admin ones plus all of Boldly, DonnaPro and Pareto.

**New pain points and triggers (not in the written stories)**
- The *specific incident* that finally made them act: an unanswered email in front of the team (Todd Dockerty, COO, BELAY); a founder leaving his wife's birthday party to buy the gift (Brad Johnson, BELAY); invoicing from the passenger seat on an anniversary trip (BELAY coach); 18-hour days that felt normal until a tweet: "there's got to be a better way" (Andrea Goulet, Boldly).
- **Admitting it is the hurdle:** "I did not want to admit that I needed help" (Luke, BELAY); "I'll just work more" (health coach, BELAY); "adding a new team member and letting go isn't easy" (Pawel, DonnaPro).
- **Loneliness of the solo founder:** "wishing I had someone to talk about the business with... It was always just me and myself" (BELAY).
- **Past VAs were task-takers:** "purely like you send an email to someone and they pump it back out to you" (Carlon Howard, BELAY); a pooled team where "tomorrow it could be virtual assistant two" (Drew Sanocki, Boldly).
- **Past hiring failed:** Eric Ritter (Pareto) "I thought it was me, right? I was burning through them."

**What they expect (video wording)**
1. *A plan, not a to-do list:* "I needed someone to come to me with a plan... walks into each weekly meeting with here's the agenda, here's the decisions I need" (Veitas Engineers, BELAY).

## 10. What this means for Pareto's project

### For the lead magnet

- **Lead with the bottleneck, not the assistant.** The emotional entry point is "I'm doing everything myself", which matches the "Are you the bottleneck?" scorecard idea (candidate idea 1 in the second brain).
- **Make the hidden cost visible.** Clients justify the spend with time-to-money math ("$50/hour work vs $10,000 work") and with family and health cost. A scorecard result or calculator should show both: hours and money lost, and the life cost.
- **Speak to the burned founder.** "Previous help failed" is the top trigger. A result type like "You've been burned before" (or the "Why your last VA failed" diagnostic, idea 4) meets them where they are.
- **Ask the qualifying questions inside the tool:** inbox volume, hours a week on admin, revenue, whether they have tried a VA before, team size.

### For ads and landing page copy

- Use their words: "doing it all", "drowning in email", "I'm the bottleneck", "see around corners", "an extension of me", "I pay for peace", "first real holiday in years".
- Pair the emotional outcome with a number. Competitors have one or the other; Pareto can have both.

### For objection handling (emails, thank-you page, call)

- **Letting go:** show staged access and a first-week plan. BELAY clients needed weeks to hand over the inbox.
- **Offshore doubts:** English, US hours, and "felt like a local employee from day one" type proof. Viva's "better than in-person EAs I've had" is the line to beat.
- **Cost:** reframe as time-to-money and peace of mind, and compare against a US hire (Pareto's offshore advantage over BELAY and Boldly).
- **Burned before:** vetting depth and the replacement guarantee, said plainly.

### For Pareto's own testimonials
Pareto's Wall of Love already proves the assistant; add the transformation. Ask current clients (or pull from the 5 video testimonials once transcribed) for: the moment they decided to hire, what they had tried before, hours a week back, what they now do with that time, and one personal outcome (family, holiday, health).
