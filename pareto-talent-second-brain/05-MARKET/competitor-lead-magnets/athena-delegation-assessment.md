# Athena — Delegation assessment

## Format
Free 10-question quiz, promoted on the homepage ("10 Questions · free", CTA "Take the free assessment"), also tagged "Executive Leverage Quiz" in Athena's email tracking.
## Promise
"How well do you actually delegate? Get a clear picture of where the gaps are, what's holding you back, and what it takes to delegate more effectively."
## Questions (paraphrased by the page reader)
1. Your last successful delegation: what made the difference? 2. A team member decides for you without checking: how do you feel? 3. You tried delegating to an assistant/contractor: what happened? 4. Two-week vacation, no phone: who covers? 5. A recurring task you meant to hand off: what's stopping you? 6. A peer pays for an EA: gut reaction? 7. A peer has an EA: first honest reaction? 8. How does your day start? 9. Last time you ended a day feeling you worked on the right things? 10. A year from now with delegation figured out: what's different?
## How they capture
The form after question 10: first name, last name, company email, how did you hear about us (all required), "Get Access". No phone, no revenue, no team size.
## How they qualify
They don't, in the quiz or the form. Everyone gets the same soft CTA.
## What happens right after opt-in (verified)
1. **On-screen thank-you page** (athena.com/delegation-assessment/thank-you), labelled "WHILE YOU WAIT". Headline: "Your result tells you where you are. An Athena EA helps you get to the next level." The result is **not shown**; the founder must wait for the email. The page sells the EA: "Most executives know they should delegate more. The gap between knowing and actually doing it comes down to having the right person"; four bullets (one dedicated, full-time EA in your time zone, employed and managed by Athena; handles work tasks, personal tasks and everything in between; gets more valuable over time as the EA builds context; top 1% of 60,000+ monthly applicants, vetted, trained, ready from day one); a testimonial from Ryan Hoover, Founder of Product Hunt ("I waited way too long to hire my EA... Ida paid for herself within the first few months and I've never looked back."); and a button **"Find your EA"** with "NO COMMITMENT REQUIRED". Note the applicant figure here (60,000+) differs from the 30–60K on the pricing page.
2. **Result email** ([[athena-quiz-result-email]]): "You scored in the Novice range", what it means, what keeps most Novices stuck, what the next level (Intermediate) looks like, and one soft link, "See how it works". No booking link, no price, no hours number.
3. Later nurture emails: not seen.
The level names in the email (Novice, Intermediate) differ from the six levels noted from the page earlier (Task-oriented ... Clairvoyant); not reconciled.
## Strengths
A personal named level; the result email explains why you are stuck and what the next level looks like; the thank-you page sells the EA right away while the founder waits; testimonial from a known founder.
## Weaknesses
No instant result on screen (the founder has to wait for an email); no qualification, so sales can't tell a $5M founder from a solopreneur; no hours-back number; no booking step in the flow (the button goes to the match flow, "Find your EA"); name, company email and source asked up front.
## What Pareto can do better
Show a score or an hours-back number on screen, add 2–3 qualifying questions (revenue, team size, timeline), route qualified founders to the Matching Call calendar, and personalise the result with what to hand off first. Use Pareto's own pains (1,500+ calls) and frameworks rather than generic levels.
