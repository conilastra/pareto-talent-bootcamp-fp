## Kasim's claims, in order
1. **Business model view:** "I think scale is bad for entrepreneurs." He builds "things that don't scale" because margin lives there: five professional-services agencies, two exits (one eight-figure), 40% margins versus 15% industry average at Solutions 8. Wants "$100 million net to me after taxes". "Gross revenue is a vanity metric." Between $1M and $3M gross he "made less money" than under $1M.
2. **The jump past about $350K** is when you must "monetize human capital the same way you monetize your own time." "The only thing that I'm really exceptional at I think is talent acquisition. It's the reason that we launched Pareto." (Also: "I've never run a Google Ads campaign end to end.")
3. **People are not commodities:** "Every business book you've ever read talks about people as though they're commodities." People are "not interchangeable at all."
4. **Why the company is called Pareto:** the 80/20 principle is fractal: of 1,000 employees, 200 do 80% of the work; of those, 40 do 80% of that; "eight people doing half of the work in a thousand-person organization"; one Solutions 8 expert (John Moran) managed almost half of gross revenue. Most companies try to flatten this ("they actually incentivize mediocrity... they're repelling top-tier talent"). See [[pareto-principle-fractal]].
5. **Pay 10% more than the high-water mark** (not 10% more than the average): the best client managers get about $120K, so offer 10% above that, "you're not getting 10% more... you're getting 5x, 10x, 100x." Treat people as adults: flexible schedule, no desktop screenshots, "managed by output", align incentives.
6. **If you cannot afford the US high-water mark, go international:** emerging-market talent is "unbelievably accessible, motivated"; "the aspirational identity internationally is working for a US company." Argentina: white-collar pay about $200 to $300 a month, strong internet, English, Western holidays; he pays his Argentine CFO "three to five times what he would make domestically."
7. **Hunters vs farmers** (from Douglas Brackmann's *Driven*): 10% of people are hunters (entrepreneurs, dopamine on the chase), 90% are farmers. "The people that you're hiring are farmers. They're not hunters. They don't want to steal your clients." (John Moran, a "$2,500 an hour" expert, stayed an employee after the exit.)
8. **Don't hire from Fiverr/Upwork/Freelancer for employees:** "you're hiring entrepreneurs, and entrepreneurs are sneaky" (they farm the work out). Instead go to local, domestic job boards, groups and LinkedIn in the target country and offer the better job. Early Pareto: "Is this a scam?" was the number-one question from applicants; now **referrals are the main source of new talent**. Kasim flew to Buenos Aires and "shook hands" with universities; his Colombian EA helped pick Argentina by analysis.

## The hiring framework (the part to reuse)
| Step | What he does |
|---|---|
| Pay | Above the market high-water mark; say why: "I pay more because I have unreasonably high expectations." |
| Job post | A sales letter with personality; first line says the subject line must read "I actually read the instructions"; instructions long enough that applicants forget the first one. |
| Fly traps | Instructions in the post (PDF resume with a naming convention, answer two questions, take a personality profile). Also checks soft skills: email formatting, communication. |
| Volume | "Our last cohort at Pareto Talent we had **2,500 applicants**. We graduated **22 EAs**." |
| Skip the interview | "Interviews are worthless. They tell you nothing." Instead a **paid trial project**: $20 for two hours; he sends the money and then does **not** send the project. Waits to see who asks. Ghosting at that point "is the ethics check". |
| Trial project design | Role-specific, hard, ambiguous, with room to go the extra mile (example: "build me a landing page for [a famous author]" and see how much research they do). Includes a deliberate trap: a wrong username/password to see whether they raise it early. Does the applicant name a deadline and manage expectations? |
| Booking test | Applicants must pick the video tool; he cancels five minutes before the call and sees who rebooks. A hothead's reply to the cancellation is also a signal. |
| Hire in groups | Several people at once, trained together: "a bunch of horses on the track", faster learning and community. |
| Two roles in one | "You're always hiring for two roles": the skill and the problem solver. |
| Not for sales | Outbound sales is the one role this model cannot fill. |

## Retention and growth
- "How do I retain peak performers? **You don't.** They want growth." Expect 18 to 24 months with men, 36 with women (his generalisation, not data to publish).
- Pay enough that money stops being the topic; then it is learning, growth and challenge. Support departures: Colin Schmeck started his own agency after two years, trained his replacement and became a referral partner. Phantom equity over real equity. "All my employees are potential business partners." Examples: Regina Bellow ran a spin-off (Starter PPC); Ivan went from a $1,000-a-month hire in Poland (spoken; see [[origin-story]]) to CTO who led the exit; his first EA became director of social, the second director of automation, the third CTO.
- **Mini-businesses from one voice note:** he asked Ivan by voice message to build a lead-gen site, CRM, follow-up and ads for self-storage; "three minutes of my time delegating, 72 hours of Ivan's time", then a $490,000 facility "valued at least $750,000" and "$260,000 in net income to me" (spoken, unverified). Also mobile-home-park lead gen and a finance-team idea ("3x financials").
- Treating staff as humans: sends money and supplies during a typhoon in the Philippines, wedding gifts; paid for a worker's IVF; helped Ivan leave Ukraine (H-1B).
